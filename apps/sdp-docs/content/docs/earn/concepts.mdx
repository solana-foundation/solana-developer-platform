## Strategies, positions and operations

- A **strategy** is one yield product: a vault, a lending market or a
  real-world-asset fund. One protocol can list several, with different assets,
  rates and withdrawal terms. [Discovery](/docs/earn/strategies) lists them.
- A **position** is one wallet's holding in one strategy. A deposit creates it;
  a [withdrawal](/docs/earn/withdrawals) names it by `positionId`.
- An **operation** is one deposit or withdrawal, from preparation through
  signing, broadcast and settlement. Every write returns one, and you poll it
  for progress.

## Who signs

SDP prepares each transaction, and the wallet you name as `source.ownerAddress`
signs it: SDP never holds its keys. The wallet signs the exact bytes SDP
prepared, and SDP rejects any change to them. Preparing grants no permission to
send: only your `/submit` call does, and only for the transaction you reviewed.

Only `completed` means the funds arrived: a signature, a confirmed request or
shares held in escrow are not a payout. If SDP can't tell yet whether something
ran, the operation stays pending until it can.

## Follow the funds

For a share-issuing strategy, the deposit moves assets into the provider's
accounts and issues shares to the owner's token account:

<EarnFlow>

- **Owner's wallet**

  Signs the reviewed transaction.

- **Provider's program and asset accounts**

  Receive the deposit and issue the strategy's shares under protocol rules.

- **Owner's share token account**

  Holds the receipt for the position. SDP's database records the activity.

</EarnFlow>

On redemption, the provider redeems the shares under its own withdrawal rules
and delivers assets to the owner. A delayed request can hold shares in escrow
while it waits; the request alone is not delivery. Earn adds no SDP omnibus
wallet or pool, but the underlying provider can pool assets, upgrade its
program or restrict redemption.

For example, Kamino's K-Vault program is
`KvauGMspG5k6rtzrqqn7WNn3oZdyKqLKwK2XWQ8FLjd` on mainnet and
`devkRngFnfp4gBc5a3LsadgbQKdPo8MSZ4prFiNSVmY` on devnet. The strategy's
`providerReference` is the vault account and its `shareMint` is the receipt
token. A deposit calls that program to mint shares to the owner; a withdrawal
burns them and pays the owner from the vault.

Before signing, you can check that the transaction calls that program and pays
into that vault; the
[program registry](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-developer-platform/blob/main/packages/sdp-types/src/kamino-programs.ts)
lists each cluster's program. After it confirms, the transaction shows the
shares the owner received. SDP runs the same checks against the strategy or
position whenever it builds a transaction.

Provider orders work differently from a vault. A WisdomTree subscription sends
USDC to an issuer-operated wallet, and its transfer agent delivers fund tokens
later. Redemption sends fund tokens back to an issuer wallet and waits for USDC.
Earn neither mints those tokens nor controls their backing. The
[fund and compliance-program registry](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-developer-platform/blob/main/packages/sdp-types/src/wisdomtree-programs.ts)
lists the supported mints and transfer hook. Check each strategy's mechanism
rather than assuming every real-world asset is a vault.

## One integration, many customers

One API key serves all your customers. Send each customer's wallet as
`source.ownerAddress` when you prepare their deposit; there is no registration
call. Withdrawals take the owner from the position. Keep the mapping from
customer to wallet in your backend, and filter reads by owner:

```http
GET /v1/earn/positions?ownerAddress=<customer-a-wallet>
GET /v1/operations?ownerAddress=<customer-a-wallet>
```

SDP isolates data by organization, project and environment, but the owner
filter is not an access boundary: your key can read every customer's records in
its project. Keep the key on your backend, authenticate each customer there and
check they own the wallet before you forward a request. A public onchain balance
does not prove the caller controls the address.
