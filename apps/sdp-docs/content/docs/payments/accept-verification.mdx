title: Verifying a payment
description: What each transfer status means, what finality means on Solana, and how often to poll.
---

When a payment lands on a wallet you control, the inbound transfer moves through a small set of
statuses. This page describes those statuses. It explains what each one says about finality on
Solana, and how to decide when it is safe to ship goods or release funds.

## Status lifecycle

The `status` field on the `Transfer` record takes one of several values. The set of values depends
on whether the record is a wallet transfer or a ramp transfer.

**Wallet transfers.** These come from `POST /v1/payments/transfers`, and from inbound transfers
that SDP discovers onchain.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `processing` | The transfer is in flight. This is the initial state of an outbound transfer at record creation. SDP is building and submitting the transaction. `signature` is not set yet. |
| `confirmed` | The cluster confirmed the transaction. `signature` and `slot` are set. `blockTime` can also be set, but it is not guaranteed at this stage. Treat it as optional until the transfer is `finalized`. |
| `finalized` | The slot that holds the transaction is locked in. This is the strongest guarantee the Solana cluster offers, and it is safe to treat as irreversible. `signature` and `slot` are set. `blockTime` is best-effort, and it can stay `null` for a transfer that the `track-pending-transfers` job finalizes. That job updates the status and the slot, but it does not backfill the block time. Resolve a definitive timestamp from `signature` through Solana RPC if you need one. |
| `failed` | SDP built and submitted the transaction, and the network rejected it. Common causes are insufficient funds, a frozen account, and an expired blockhash. The `error` field describes what reached the chain. |

An outbound wallet transfer moves from `processing` to `confirmed` to `finalized`. An inbound
transfer that SDP discovers through onchain signature history appears directly at `confirmed`, or
at `failed`. An inbound transfer never appears as `processing`.

**Ramp transfers.** A [Ramp Quote](/docs/payments/ramps) creates these.

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `pending` | SDP created the quote. No funding has been detected yet, and there is no `signature`. |
| `awaiting_payment` | The quote's delivery mode is `manual_instructions`. SDP is waiting for the payer to fund the account described in the quote. |
| `settling` | The provider detected the payment, or started to process it. |
| `completed` | The provider reports that the ramp settled. |
| `canceled` | You canceled the transfer with `POST /v1/payments/ramps/transfers/cancel` before it was funded. |
| `expired` | The funding window elapsed and no payment arrived. |
| `failed` | The provider rejected or reversed the ramp. The `error` field describes why, when SDP sets it. |

Any state can move to `failed`. The terminal states are `failed`, `completed`, `canceled`, and
`expired`.

## Finality on Solana

For real-money flows, treat `finalized` as the safe-to-act-on signal.

`confirmed` is durable enough for most workflows that are not adversarial, such as showing a paid
badge in your own dashboard. A large enough cluster reorganization can in theory roll back a
`confirmed` transaction. This does not happen to a `finalized` transaction.

Use `confirmed` to release low-stakes side effects, such as a status email or a screen update. Use
`finalized` for irreversible side effects, such as releasing custody, shipping high-value goods, or
posting to your general ledger.

## Polling cadence

SDP does not emit settlement webhooks today. You observe a status by polling:

- `GET /v1/payments/transfers/{id}` returns the current state of one transfer.
- `GET /v1/payments/transfers?direction=inbound&from=…` returns a windowed list. See
  [Indexing and reconciliation](/docs/payments/accept-indexing).

The right cadence depends on how quickly you need to see a state change, and on how large your
retry budget is. These ranges are a reasonable starting point:

- **Active checkout**, where the customer is watching a payment screen. Poll every 2 to 5 seconds
  for up to a minute, then back off.
- **Background reconciliation**, where nobody is waiting. Poll every 15 to 30 seconds, or on each
  worker tick.
- **Long-tail catch-up**, where you read a status after the fact. Read once when your application
  needs to act. Do not run a polling loop.

The backend team plans to tighten these numbers once it confirms a target. Treat the ranges above
as defensible defaults today.

## Independent verification

Every transfer that reaches `processing` or a later state carries enough information to verify
against the network directly:

- **`signature`** is the Solana transaction signature. Paste it into [Solscan](https://solscan.io/)
  or [Solana Explorer](https://explorer.solana.com/) to see the onchain record.
- **`slot`** is the slot that the transaction landed in.
- **`blockTime`** is the ISO 8601 timestamp of slot finality.
- **`fee`** is the fee paid, in lamports.

If you run your own indexer with `getSignatureStatuses` against a Solana RPC endpoint,
cross-reference the SDP `signature` against your indexer. That confirms that the SDP status matches
what your own nodes see.

## Risk metadata

When a risk-screening provider is configured on the organization, a transfer can carry a `risk`
field. That field holds `provider`, `score`, `level`, and `evaluatedAt`. The `level` value is
`low`, `medium`, `high`, or `unknown`. Use it to gate downstream side effects on a risk threshold.
The field is absent when no risk provider is configured.

Risk evaluation does not block a transfer by default. A transfer can complete with a high risk
score. To halt a transfer at a risk threshold, enforce that in your own application logic on top of
the `risk.level` value.

## Failure handling

When `status` settles to `failed`:

- Read the `error` field. SDP returns the chain-level error message. Common causes are insufficient
  funds on the source wallet, an account frozen by the token authority, and a stale blockhash.
- Decide whether to retry. The original transfer record is terminal. A retry creates a new transfer
  with a new ID, and eventually a new signature.
- Report the failure to whoever needs to know, such as the merchant, the customer, or your
  operations team.
