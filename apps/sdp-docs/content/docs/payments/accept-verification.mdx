A transfer's `status` tells you whether a payment landed and whether it can still be undone. This page covers each status, when a payment is safe to act on, and how to check it against the chain.

## Status lifecycle

A wallet transfer, sent or received, has one of these statuses:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `processing` | Outbound and in flight: SDP is signing and submitting it, or waiting for the chain. `signature` can still be `null`. |
| `confirmed` | The transaction landed in a block that a supermajority of the cluster voted for. `signature` and `slot` are set. Not yet irreversible. |
| `finalized` | The block is finalized. Irreversible. |
| `failed` | The transfer never reached the network, or the network rejected it. The recipient was not paid; `error` says why. |

`POST /v1/payments/transfers` waits for finality, so a successful request usually answers `finalized`. If the wait runs out, it answers `processing`, and SDP moves the transfer to `confirmed`, `finalized` or `failed` as the chain settles. A `processing` transfer that never got a `signature` fails after 5 minutes, unless it is waiting on a custody approval.

Inbound payments differ. A paid payment request is recorded at `finalized`. An observed row (from `includeObserved=true`) shows `confirmed` or `failed` and never `finalized`, because SDP rebuilds it from chain history on each read. Check its finality yourself; see [Independent verification](#independent-verification).

`finalized` and `failed` are final. Transfers that other modules create document their own lifecycle.

## Finality on Solana

Act on `finalized` for anything irreversible: releasing custody, shipping high-value goods, posting to your general ledger. A `confirmed` transaction has a supermajority vote but can, rarely, still be dropped. Use it for low-stakes steps, such as a status email or a "paid" badge.

## Polling cadence

SDP sends no settlement webhooks, so poll:

- `GET /v1/payments/transfers/{transferId}` for one recorded transfer.
- `GET /v1/payments/transfers` for a time window or one wallet's activity. See [Indexing and reconciliation](/docs/payments/accept-indexing).

Start from these ranges:

- **Active checkout** (someone is watching the payment screen): every 2–5 seconds for up to a minute, then back off.
- **Background reconciliation** (no one is waiting): every 15–30 seconds, or on each worker tick.
- **After the fact**: one read when your application needs the answer, with no loop.

Every poll counts against your key's [rate limit](/docs/reference/conventions/rate-limits). Reads with `includeObserved=true` also count against their own quota of 30 a minute per API key, so spread them across your open orders.

## Independent verification

Once a transfer has a `signature`, you can check it against the network yourself:

- **`signature`**: open it on [Solana Explorer](https://explorer.solana.com/), or look it up with `getSignatureStatuses` on your own RPC.
- **`slot`**: the slot the transaction landed in.
- **`blockTime`**: the block's timestamp (ISO 8601). It is often `null` on transfers SDP sends; `confirmedAt` records when SDP saw the transaction land.
- **`fee`**: the network fee in lamports, when known.

If you run your own indexer, compare its view of each `signature` with SDP's `status`.

## Failure handling

When `status` is `failed`:

- Read `error` for the reason.
- Decide whether to try again. The failed transfer stays failed, and its `Idempotency-Key` replays the failure. To retry, send a new request with a new key; it creates a new transfer with its own `id` and `signature`.
- Tell whoever needs to know: the merchant, the customer, your operations team.
